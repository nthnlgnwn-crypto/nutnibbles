Saawaan Bangkok

Beautiful craft, not automatic return.

7.2/10

  • Date night
  • Bangkok fine dining
  • Tasting menu

Dinner date, Bangkok, set menu. Polished technique, great service, a few excellent highs. Larb and Fermented stuck with me most. Raw was the weakest. Overall a careful 7.2/10.

I respected the work at Saawaan straight away. Not every bite made me want to come back, though.

Dinner date in Bangkok, 17 Jan 2026. Polished room, hospitable waiters, smooth service all through the set menu. As a night out, easy to enjoy.

On the food, I’m more careful. Go if you want refined Thai fine dining with strong craft and a few memorable moments. If you’re choosing purely on taste, I’d still try other Bangkok restaurants before going back to this one.

Where the Craft Showed Most

The plates always looked confident, even when the flavor was more low-key.

Every plate looked interesting. Good color, good textures, nothing overdone, even on courses I didn’t love.

Dramatic plating

The more theatrical courses made it feel most like an occasion. Here it felt like a destination dinner, not just a long line of small plates.

Refined bowl

Some of the most careful courses came in deep bowls with one neat garnish in the middle. That precision is part of why it felt like real fine dining, not just styling.

Color, nothing overdone

The craft was clearest in the small stuff: how things played off each other, where the garnish goes, and every element having a reason to be on the plate.

Best Moments

Larb and Fermented were the clearest highs.

Here the ideas felt most complete. Interesting, precise, and satisfying beyond just looking good.

Larb

Jean-Paul oyster with roselle, Surin sato, pakpaew, and makwan. One of the two courses I’d tell people about first. The flavors were layered and complete, not just pretty.

Fermented

Ratchaburi pork trotter with fresh turmeric. The other clear high of the night, level with Larb. Rich, confident, and memorable in a way several of the courses around it weren’t.

Raw was the weakest dish for me: Ranong blue swimmer crab with fresh mango and Phang Nga cashew nuts. It didn’t ruin the meal. It just didn’t have the confidence of those two.

The Measured Part

Some dishes were more thoughtful than crave-worthy.

This is why my review is more careful. Nothing was awkward to eat, but I wasn’t equally excited about every course.

Chilled presentation

A cooler, cleaner course. I liked the mix of temperature and texture, but it’s not a flavor I still think about.

Composed course

Some plates looked perfect but came across more as careful fine dining than as flavors I still think about. That kept happening through the middle of the menu.

Steamed squid, Boiled beef and Curry all had strong technique, but taste-wise the dinner was “good” more often than “must remember.” That’s why it’s a 7.2 and not a favorite.

Sweet Finish

Neat ending, but the savory side was better.

Dessert was polished and suited the room. What I remember most is still the savory highs, not the sweet ending.

Dessert / sweet finish

Black rice with makwan, Ki-Lo, and perilla seed, plus petit fours on the table. A proper ending to a long tasting menu, but I wouldn’t rank the sweets above the best savory courses.

Final Verdict

Worth considering, but not the Bangkok meal I crave most.

Saawaan is a good pick if you want Bangkok fine dining with a polished room, hospitable service and obvious craft. Not a disappointment. Not an automatic repeat either.

Expensive, but fair enough for the setting and the work on the plate. My hesitation is simple: taste-wise, the whole dinner wasn’t as memorable as its best courses.

Worth it? For a date or a foodie dinner, if you already like tasting menus and want something polished. If you only get one fine-dining night in Bangkok and want the most flavor, look at a few more options first.

Disclosure: I paid for this meal in full. Nothing here was hosted, comped, or discounted.
