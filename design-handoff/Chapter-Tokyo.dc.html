Tokyo felt like the last deep breath of the trip: still busy and full, but carried by enough comfort, food, and quieter pauses to make the ending feel complete instead of rushed.

On 26 Jan we flew into Tokyo after Kansai. After snowy Sapporo, louder Osaka, and the Kyoto kimono day, Tokyo felt like the final all-rounder: good meals, neighbourhood wandering, hotel comfort, and the little stops that make a last city satisfying rather than hurried.

What worked best was the balance. Tokyo still gave us the big-city energy, but it also gave us slower moments that made the chapter easier to remember clearly: a hotel that felt like a proper reset, a food stop worth its own note, and a few gentle detours before the trip closed.

The stay · Cerulean Tower Tokyu Hotel

The hotel that quietly became the trip’s best stay

It is on the older side, but that did not work against it here: the room felt spacious, the facilities were genuinely useful, and the stay gave the final city a more comfortable base.

The gym especially stood out because it felt properly equipped, not like a token hotel corner. After several cities of moving around, that kind of practical comfort mattered more than a newer-looking room would have.

Personal take

Older hotel, but spacious rooms and strong facilities. If comfort matters near the end of a busy Japan route, this was the most satisfying stay of the trip.

26 Jan · Late lunch · Gyukatsu Motomura

Tokyo started with the best gyukatsu

Exactly the kind of first meal I like after moving cities: focused, satisfying, and memorable without needing a plan. The crispy cutlet, small sides, rice bowls and hot stone made the meal feel active instead of routine.

Why it works
Rich, warm and immediately satisfying after a travel day.

27 Jan · Fuji-san one-day tour

Picture hunting around Mt. Fuji

The weather was moody, with clouds moving around the mountain, so the day became less of a guaranteed postcard stop and more of a patient search for the right opening.

Even when the peak was hiding, Fuji-san still looked dramatic. Snow, dark clouds, village rooftops, lake reflections and sudden clear moments made every stop feel like a different version of the same mountain.

The clouds made it less predictable — and that made each clear view feel earned.

28 Jan · Shibuya Cat Lounge MOCHA

A soft pause before heading back into the city

After the bigger sightseeing days this felt like a calm indoor break: warm lights, wood textures, shelves of manga, and cats casually taking over the lounge like they owned it.

What stood out

Feeding time pulled the whole room into one calm little circle instead of a chaotic tourist crowd.

28 Jan · Meiji Jingu, Harajuku, I’m donut?

A calm shrine walk before the city lights returned

The shrine grounds felt peaceful and spacious — sake barrels, tall trees, wooden ema prayers, and the kind of quiet that makes the city feel much farther away than it is.

After that, Harajuku and Omotesando brought the city back in: reflective buildings, sunset light, and a stop at I’m donut? for the kind of small snack mission that makes Tokyo walking feel purposeful.
