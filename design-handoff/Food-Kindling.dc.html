Kindling feels special because it does not chase a cold fine-dining mood. It feels intimate, slightly jadul in a good way, and quietly confident enough to let the evening unfold slowly.

We moved through three rooms across the night: one for the appetizers, one for the mains, and one for dessert. That small change in setting made the dinner feel more like a real occasion — it resets your attention instead of leaving you at one table for three hours.

The menu described itself as a quiet dialogue between Chinese roots, French refinement, and the terroir of Indonesia. For me it landed best when it leaned into comfort, depth, and a sense of occasion rather than pure novelty.

The dishes that shaped the night

The menu moved between small bites, seafood, duck, steak and dessert, but the strongest parts were the courses that balanced comfort with a little ceremony — the ones that made the night feel personal rather than simply technical.

Weak spot

The firefly squid was the one course that did not fully land — too fishy for my preference. Everything else stayed strong enough that I still left very satisfied.

The room changes made it feel ceremonial

Kindling’s personality comes through as much in the setting as in the food. It is the kind of place where the room itself helps you slow down, which is exactly what I want from a birthday dinner.

Less like a formal dining room, more like being guided through a very thoughtful birthday evening.
