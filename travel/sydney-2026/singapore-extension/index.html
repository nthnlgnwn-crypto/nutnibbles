The Singapore extension

A volcano in the Sunda Strait ended the trip, and then didn’t. This chapter is the four days a journey spent refusing to close, and what you do inside them.

  • Singapore
  • 5–8 Sep 2026
  • Unplanned
  • Krakatau
  • Four days

Two free days in Singapore is a story you tell at dinner. Day three onwards, paying for it yourself, is just an expense with a nice view. Both versions happened on the same trip, about forty-eight hours apart.

The flight home routed through Singapore. The Singapore–Jakarta leg was cancelled: volcanic ash from Krakatau.

Singapore Airlines put us up at the Royal Plaza on Scotts, with breakfast, lunch and dinner buffets included. For two days this was, frankly, a gift. Somebody else’s hotel, somebody else’s meals, and a city I like anyway.

There are worse ways for a trip to refuse to end. And then the coverage ran out.

The Singapore extension

5 Sep

A volcano in the Sunda Strait ended the trip, and then didn’t

Out of Sydney in the evening, into Singapore, and into the news that the last leg was not happening. The airline handled it well — a hotel on Scotts Road with all three meals included — and for two days that was, frankly, a gift.

6 Sep

Checked out at nine, checked back in by lunchtime

Out of the hotel around nine to head for Changi, with a rebooked flight around noon. Then the news that Soekarno-Hatta was still closed for the day. Checked back in, and assembled a day out of a day that wasn’t supposed to exist.

7–8 Sep

The part where it stops being a gift

The airline’s coverage ran out. The last two days were on my own money, in a city I hadn’t planned to be in, waiting on an airport in another country to reopen.

Three rebookings and a Tuesday afternoon

The return was first pushed all the way out to the 12th, which is the number that made this feel less like a delay and more like a relocation. Then it moved forward to the 9th. Then Soekarno-Hatta reopened on the 8th, and Singapore Airlines put me on the 13:10 that same afternoon. I got home on Tuesday 8 September, four days later than the ticket said and four days earlier than I had made peace with.

If it happens to you

Take the airline hotel and the buffet without guilt, and use the free days properly — they are the only part of this you will remember fondly. Then start counting, because the coverage ends before the disruption does. And don’t plan around the rebooked date: mine moved three times in three days.

Honest downside

An extended trip you did not choose is not a bonus holiday. Two days of it is a story. After that it is a hotel bill, a laundry problem, and a daily check of whether an airport in another country has reopened.
