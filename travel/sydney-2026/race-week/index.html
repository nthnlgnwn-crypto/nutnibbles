Australia / Sydney / 26–30 Aug 2026

Race week

Five days that were entirely about one Sunday morning: getting there, running easy, collecting a number with my name on it, lying very still, and then forty-two kilometres.

  • TCS Sydney Marathon
  • World Marathon Major
  • Bib 17821
  • Wave start 6:50
  • 6 hours 22 minutes

This is the honest version. I signed up because my gym friends were going, I did not train, and the course was hillier than I had accounted for. All three of those facts show up in the finish time.

Sydney is a World Marathon Major and I picked it because it is the closest one to Indonesia.

That is not a reason anybody puts in a race report, but it is the true one. My gym friends were going — this was their fifth or sixth major — and it was my first anything.

I want to be exact about the preparation, because “didn’t train much” is the kind of soft phrasing that makes this sound better than it was. Work took the year. My actual preparation was the last two or three weeks: running two or three times a week, longest run 10km. For a 42km race.

Race day, 30 August

26–27 Aug

Eight hours in Singapore, which I did not yet know was practice

The trip out went Jakarta–Singapore–Sydney with an eight-hour transit in the middle. There is no way to make an eight-hour layover interesting and I am not going to try. It is the tax you pay for the cheaper routing, and I paid it.

28 Aug

Five kilometres with people who knew what they were doing

A shakeout run with my gym friends, out toward Macquarie Point, about 5km. They are the reason I was in Sydney at all. I turned back before they did, because I had a race pack to collect.

29 Aug

Coffee, one disappointing pastry, and lying very still

Morning coffee at Alma. Then AP House, where the pastries were the letdown of the day — I had built them up and they didn’t hold. Lunch at Tao, then back to the hotel to do nothing on purpose.

30 Aug

I ran my first marathon on three weeks of training. It went about as well as that sounds.

Left the hotel at 5am. Wave start at 6:50. Finished in 6 hours 22 minutes, on a course that turned out to be properly hilly, with elevation I hadn’t planned for because I hadn’t planned for anything. Afterwards: back to the hotel, then Doo Dee King for something light, then a proper dinner at Kagoshima Master Ultimo, which was very good and which I have no photographs of, because by that point I had stopped documenting anything.

Worth it?

Yes, and I am still not satisfied with that time. Both are true. I finished a major on almost no preparation, which I am glad about, and I know exactly why the number looks the way it does, which is the part I would change. The next one I train for properly.

What didn’t work

The training, obviously — but specifically the hills. A 10km longest run tells you nothing about what a hilly 42km does to your legs at kilometre 25. That was the gap, and it was entirely mine.
