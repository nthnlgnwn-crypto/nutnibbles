Australia / Sydney / 26–30 Aug 2026

Race week

Five days, all about one Sunday morning: getting there, an easy run, picking up a number with my name on it, lying very still, then forty-two kilometres.

  • TCS Sydney Marathon
  • World Marathon Major
  • Bib 17821
  • Wave start 6:50
  • 6 hours 22 minutes

Short version: I signed up because my gym friends were going, I didn’t train, and the course was hillier than I expected. You can see all three in the finish time.

Sydney is a World Marathon Major, and I picked it because it’s the closest one to Indonesia.

Not a very inspiring reason, but it’s the real one. My gym friends were going. It was their fifth or sixth major. It was my first anything.

“Didn’t train much” makes it sound better than it was, so here are the details. Work took the year. My actual training was the last two or three weeks: running two or three times a week, longest run 10km. For a 42km race.

Race day, 30 August

26–27 Aug

Eight hours in Singapore. Turns out that was practice

The trip out went Jakarta–Singapore–Sydney with an eight-hour transit in the middle. An eight-hour layover is boring and I won’t pretend otherwise. That’s the price of the cheaper route.

28 Aug

Five kilometres with people who knew what they were doing

Shakeout run with my gym friends, out toward Macquarie Point, about 5km. They’re the reason I was in Sydney at all. I turned back before they did because I had a race pack to collect.

29 Aug

Coffee, one disappointing pastry, and lying very still

Morning coffee at Alma. Then AP House, where the pastries were the letdown of the day. I’d built them up and they didn’t deliver. Lunch at Tao, then back to the hotel to do nothing on purpose.

30 Aug

I ran my first marathon on three weeks of training. It went about as well as that sounds.

Left the hotel at 5am. Wave start at 6:50. Finished in 6 hours 22 minutes. The course was properly hilly, and I hadn’t planned for the hills because I hadn’t planned for anything. After: back to the hotel, then Doo Dee King for something light, then a proper dinner at Kagoshima Master Ultimo. Very good, and no photos, because by then I’d stopped taking any.

Worth it?

Yes. I’m still not satisfied with that time, but I finished a major on almost no preparation and I’m glad about that. I know exactly why the number looks like that. The next one I train for properly.

What didn’t work

The training, obviously. Mostly the hills. A 10km longest run tells you nothing about what a hilly 42km does to your legs at kilometre 25. That one’s on me.
