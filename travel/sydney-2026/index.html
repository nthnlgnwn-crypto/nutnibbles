Australia & Singapore / 26 Aug – 8 Sep 2026

I ran my first marathon on three weeks of training, then a volcano wouldn’t let me go home.

Sydney was meant to be a race and a week of eating. It became a race, a week of eating, and an unplanned residency in Singapore courtesy of an ash cloud over the Sunda Strait.

Route

Out through Singapore, and back into it

An eight-hour transit in Singapore on the way out felt long at the time and now reads as foreshadowing. Nine days in Sydney built around race day on the 30th. Then a return through Singapore that took four days to finish.

Go when

Late August is race season and the weather helps

Sydney in late August was comfortable to run in and comfortable to walk around afterwards, and the beaches were still quiet enough to lie on without negotiating for space.

Best meal

Sydney Fish Market, and it isn’t close

Sea urchin and oysters. DOPA is the best sit-down meal of the trip; Saga Bar is the best evening. None of the restaurant dinners beat standing at the market.

What to skip

Don’t force a last viewpoint into a full day

We dropped Observatory Hill after five because we were tired, and the day was better for it. One more stop is how you end up enjoying none of them.

Book early

Race entry, and the day after

The entry is the obvious one. The less obvious one: if you are running, put nothing in the day after the race. You will not want it there.

Train

If you are doing a major, do the work

Three weeks and a 10km long run gets you a finish and a time you will be explaining afterwards. It is survivable. It is not advisable.

Honest downside

The course is hillier than the postcards suggest

Sydney reads flat in photographs of the harbour. It is not. The elevation was the part I had least planned for, mostly because I had planned for nothing.
